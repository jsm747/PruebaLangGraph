# Skill — design session (Architect, Builder)

How a session with the owner or a member is run. Applies to project design, process-definition changes, re-planning conversations and instantiation.

## Rules of the session
1. **One topic at a time.** Name the topic; explore it; settle it; the owner closes it explicitly; only then the next. Never a list of decisions for batch approval; never two open topics.
2. **Their frame first.** Before any option is offered, restate the owner's view of the topic in their terms and get it accepted. Options offered before this contaminate the exploration.
3. **Challenge before nuance.** If the owner's reasoning has a gap, say it first and plainly; qualifications after.
4. **Verify in session.** Any claim about an existing document, number, person or system is checked against it during the session (literal search, directory lookup, the file itself). What cannot be checked is written as UNKNOWN with who will verify it.
5. **No plausible filling.** Where information is missing, the output is a question or an explicit *free*; never a reasonable guess presented as a fact.
6. **Decisions are written as they close**, with the owner named, before the next topic opens. The transcript is the anchor of each record.
7. **Authority.** A decision closes only with the person who holds it; what a member without authority contributes is recorded as a draft or an open question, and the holder is asked.
8. **Process blocks are not chased here.** A block, delay or missing approval that surfaces in a content session is filed as a signal through the process door.
9. **Corrections are kept.** Every correction the owner makes to the Architect's or Builder's proposal is written to the corrections ledger verbatim; the retrospective files the durable ones to the user layer.
10. **End with the state.** The session ends with the list of closed topics, open topics and who owns each; nothing stays only in the conversation.

## Shape of a topic
*Restate* (their frame) → *verify* (what exists) → *propose* (one proposal, with what it rules out) → *settle* (the owner's decision, verbatim) → *write* (record, contract field or plan line).

## Signs the session is going wrong
Several decisions in one message · an option offered before the restatement was accepted · a claim about state without an anchor · "we can decide that later" written as if it were a decision · a topic reopened after the owner closed it.
